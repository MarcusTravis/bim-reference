Revit add-ins

The add-ins worth knowing if you work in Revit: pyRevit, DiRoots, Ideate, CTC, and others that save daily clicks.

Data last checked: Nov 13, 2026 · What changed

Which Revit add-ins are most widely used?

In the Confluence 2025 tech-stack inventory, pyRevit was listed by 90 firms, AVAIL by 68, DiRoots Tools by 63, and Ideate Explorer by 51. CTC BIM Manager Suite (42 firms) and RevitLookup (50) are also common. Several of the most-used add-ins are free or have a free tier.

How the ratings work

  • Popularity is a 1–5 judgment (halves allowed) of how commonly a product shows up in recent firm surveys, review directories, or marketplace ranks — relative to the other products on this page, not a computed formula.
  • Captions name the source and a “checked Nov 2026” date. If there was no usable source, the caption says “Editorial estimate.”
  • Learning curve is editorial (Gentler / Moderate / Steep) based on typical new-user experience, not a test score.
  • Cost is a five-step scale (Free / $ / $$ / $$$ / $$$$). See “How cost is rated” for the band definitions on this page. Captions cite a source or say the rating is an estimate.
  • Scores are relative to the other Revit add-ins on this page.

How cost is rated

  • Cost uses a published annual price for one commercial seat when we could find one this session: the cheapest official annual rate, or monthly × 12 if only monthly is listed. Perpetual licenses are rated on the first-year purchase price.
  • Free — no license fee for the core product (open source or free commercial use).
  • $ — under about $500 / year.
  • $$ — about $500–2,000 / year.
  • $$$ — about $2,000–5,000 / year.
  • $$$$ — about $5,000+ / year, or quote-only enterprise pricing with no public list price.
  • If the vendor only quotes, or no public figure turned up, the caption says so and the band is an editorial estimate — never an invented dollar amount. Always confirm current pricing with the vendor.

Sources and limits

Primary source: the same Confluence 2025 inventory (counts of firms indicating use). Autodesk App Store ranks and older review recaps are cited where the inventory is silent (notably Bird Tools and historical COINS Auto-Section Box reviews). COINS has since been reported removed from the App Store — that status is noted on the card. Xrev Transmit and AGACAD are editorial estimates because no 2025–2026 firm count was found this session.

Independence

This site is ad-free and has no affiliate links. Official-site buttons go straight to the vendor. Stars are not paid placement.

Frequently asked questions

What is the most popular Revit add-in?

On this chart it is pyRevit. 90 firms listed it in the Confluence 2025 inventory, among the highest counts for Revit utilities in that survey. It is free and open source.

Are there free Revit add-ins worth installing?

Yes. pyRevit and RevitLookup are free and open source. AVAIL has a free single-user tier, and DiRoots has a free feature-limited tier. RevitLookup is aimed at power users and developers.

What happened to COINS Auto-Section Box?

It was historically a top Autodesk App Store download, and 34 firms listed it in Confluence 2025. It has since been reported removed from the Autodesk App Store, and no current public price was found.

Why are some add-ins marked “Editorial estimate”?

Xrev Transmit and AGACAD are marked as estimates because no 2025–2026 firm count was found for them. Ratings are relative to the other Revit add-ins on this page.
