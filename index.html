What is the BIM Reference Library?

bimchart.net is a free, ad-free set of comparison charts for the software and reality-capture hardware used to design, coordinate, fabricate, and visualize buildings. Each section is one scrollable chart with a row per product. You can compare popularity, cost, learning curve, and pros and cons, and each row links to the vendor’s official site.

    How to use this site

    Search above to jump straight to any product’s row, or open a section. Each section opens as a compact, scrollable comparison chart — one row per product — that you can filter, search, and sort. Stars are a 1–5 popularity judgment with a source caption. Cost uses a Free / $ / $$ / $$$ / $$$$ scale with a source caption. Learning-curve labels are editorial. Official-site links are not affiliate links.

    Where the numbers come from

    The main firm-adoption source for software is the Confluence 2025 AEC Tech Stack Survey (Q4 2025). European figures for Archicad and ALLPLAN come from the European Architectural Barometer Q4 2025. Review counts come from G2, checked in November 2026. Scanning and drone pages have no comparable product-level firm survey in that report, so popularity there is an editorial judgment unless a caption cites another source. If a product has no usable source, the caption says “Editorial estimate.”

    What this is not

    This is not a vendor ranking you should buy from blindly. It is a starting map for people learning the BIM software landscape.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is bimchart.net free, and are the links paid?

    Yes, it is free. The site has no ads and no affiliate links. Official-site links go straight to the vendor, and star ratings are not paid placement.

    Where do the popularity ratings come from?

    For software, the main source is the Confluence 2025 AEC Tech Stack Survey (Q4 2025). Archicad and ALLPLAN also cite the European Architectural Barometer Q4 2025, and G2 review counts were checked in November 2026. That survey has no product-level scanner or drone figures, so popularity on those pages is an editorial estimate. Every rating has a caption naming its source, or it says “Editorial estimate.”

    How is cost rated?

    Cost uses a five-step scale: Free, $, $$, $$$, and $$$$. Software is rated on a published annual price for one commercial seat. Scanners and drones are rated on a purchase-price band for one unit or kit. Each section explains its bands. If a vendor only gives quotes, the caption marks the band as an estimate. No dollar amounts are made up.

    Is the learning curve a measured score?

    No. Learning curve (Gentler, Moderate, or Steep) is an editorial rating based on what new users typically go through. It is not a test score.

    How current is the data?

    Product data was last checked on the date shown at the top of each section page (November 2026). The Updates page lists every change in plain English.
