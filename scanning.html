Scanning hardware

The terrestrial, SLAM, and handheld scanners BIM teams actually take to site for as-builts and scan-to-BIM — from Leica and FARO tripods to NavVis wearables and Matterport.

Which laser scanners do BIM teams use for scan-to-BIM?

Most BIM as-built work uses one of three scanner types. Terrestrial tripod scanners (Leica RTC360, FARO Focus, Trimble X9) give the most controlled captures. Mobile SLAM scanners (Leica BLK2GO, NavVis VLX 3, FARO Orbis) are faster to walk through interiors. Compact kits (Leica BLK360, Matterport Pro3) are lighter and cheaper to start with. Popularity on this page is an editorial judgment because no product-level scanner survey was found.

How the ratings work

  • Popularity is a 1–5 judgment (halves allowed) of how commonly a product shows up in recent firm surveys, review directories, or marketplace ranks — relative to the other products on this page, not a computed formula.
  • Captions name the source and a “checked Nov 2026” date. If there was no usable source, the caption says “Editorial estimate.”
  • Learning curve is editorial (Gentler / Moderate / Steep) based on typical new-user experience, not a test score.
  • Cost is a five-step scale (Free / $ / $$ / $$$ / $$$$). See “How cost is rated” for the band definitions on this page. Captions cite a source or say the rating is an estimate.
  • Scores are relative to the other scanners on this page, not a universal ranking of all survey instruments.

How cost is rated

  • Cost on this page is a purchase-price band for one unit, or a clearly described kit, not an annual software seat. Always confirm current pricing with the vendor.
  • $ — under about $15,000.
  • $$ — about $15,000–40,000.
  • $$$ — about $40,000–80,000.
  • $$$$ — about $80,000+, or quote-only with no public list price found this session.
  • Captions cite an official shop, an authorized reseller listing, or say the band is an estimate. No dollar amounts were invented.

Sources and limits

The Confluence 2025 AEC Tech Stack Survey covers software only and has no product-level scanner penetration figures. A BuiltWorlds 2026 Field Solutions reality-capture report found that among organizations already using reality capture, LiDAR scanners were used by 81% (drones 100%, 360 photography 88%) — a category-level figure, not a product share. Popularity on this page is therefore an editorial judgment, with no invented firm-percentage or review counts. Cost uses a purchase-price band for one unit; official Leica shop prices and clearly labeled reseller listings are cited where they could be checked in November 2026. Quote-only products are marked as estimates. Specs in the Type, Range, Accuracy, and Scan speed columns are taken from official vendor pages checked this session, or left qualitative when a number was not on the page.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a laser scanner for BIM cost?

Prices vary a lot by class. The official Leica shop lists the RTC360 (“Leica RTC”) at $46,000 and the BLK360 at $26,500. Matterport Pro3 starts at $1,995, plus a required Professional subscription starting at $245 a month. Many other scanners are quote-only, so their bands are marked as estimates.

What is the difference between a tripod scanner and a SLAM scanner?

A terrestrial tripod scanner captures from fixed stations for controlled, registered scans. A mobile SLAM scanner is carried, handheld or wearable, and captures while you walk. That is faster for interiors, stairs, and large buildings where tripod setups are slow.

Which scanners are best for building interiors?

On this chart, the Leica BLK2GO, NavVis VLX 3, FARO ZEB Horizon, and NavVis MLX carry the Interiors tag. All four are mobile SLAM scanners.

Why is scanner popularity an editorial estimate?

The Confluence 2025 survey covers software only. A BuiltWorlds 2026 reality-capture report found that 81% of organizations already using reality capture used LiDAR scanners, but that is a category figure, not a brand share. No firm percentages or review counts were made up.
